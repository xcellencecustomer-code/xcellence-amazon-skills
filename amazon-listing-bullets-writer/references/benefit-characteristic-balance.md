# Benefit, Characteristic, and Objection Balance

Use this guide to decide what belongs in each bullet.

## Priority order

1. Benefits
2. Useful characteristics
3. Objection handling

## Why benefits lead

Benefits create interest and desire. They answer:
- what gets easier
- what improves
- what pain is reduced
- what result becomes possible

## Why characteristics still matter

Characteristics support credibility. They prove or explain the benefit.

Use this transformation pattern:
characteristic -> practical advantage -> customer benefit

Example:
- thick silicone -> more resistant -> safer and longer-lasting use

## Why objections matter

A buyer can want the product and still hesitate.

Typical objections include:
- will it work
- will it last
- is it easy to use
- is it really suitable
- is it worth the price
- what if I am disappointed

Use objections naturally:
- inside concrete bullets when the doubt is practical
- inside differentiation when the doubt is comparative
- inside the last bullet when the doubt is about risk
