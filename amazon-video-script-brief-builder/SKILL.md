# Amazon Video Script Brief Builder

Build Amazon video briefs and scripts as short conversion sequences. The goal is not to create a pretty product video. The goal is to structure a video that helps a shopper understand the product quickly, see why it matters, believe the promise, and feel safer buying.

## Overview

Use this skill for Amazon video planning that must balance:
- speed of comprehension
- benefit communication
- scene utility
- objection handling
- differentiation
- silent viewing behavior
- mobile readability
- conversion-first sequencing

This skill is for video strategy, briefing, and script structure. It creates an actionable brief for editors, creators, videographers, or downstream creative systems.

## Core rules

- Treat the video as a compressed persuasion sequence, not as a brand film.
- The product and its value must appear early.
- Do not let the brand intro delay the selling message.
- Every scene must have a commercial function.
- A scene is useful only if it:
  - demonstrates a benefit
  - proves a promise
  - reduces a buying doubt
  - shows real use
  - clarifies why this product is worth choosing
- Translate characteristics into visible customer benefits.
- Prefer scenes that show over lines that merely claim.
- Keep the video understandable without sound.
- Use short, scannable overlays when text is needed.
- Remove decorative scenes that do not improve conversion.

## Mandatory input transparency

Before producing a final brief or script, determine which of these cases applies.

### Case 1: strong strategic inputs are available

Best inputs include:
- product definition
- title or central promise
- bullet points or key benefits
- ideal customer
- objections
- differentiators
- category context
- existing visual assets or filming constraints

When these are available:
- use them as the main decision layer
- rank the strongest messages
- convert those messages into scenes
- structure the sequence for attention, proof, and reassurance
- write a complete brief or short script

### Case 2: some important inputs are missing

Before producing the final brief, explicitly say that:
- the video brief will be provisional
- some scene choices are being inferred from product logic and Amazon best practice
- the result should be refined when customer, objections, benefits, or bullet structure are known

Use direct language. Do not hide this limitation.

## Default video logic

Use this default order unless the category requires an adjusted structure:

1. Hook with product value or strongest use case
2. Main benefit
3. Demonstration of use
4. Secondary benefit or differentiator
5. Objection handling or reassurance
6. Final confidence cue with product and brand support

For very short videos, compress but preserve the logic:
- attract
- explain
- prove
- reassure

Use the guidance in `references/video-sequence-framework.md`.

## Scene logic

For every scene, define:
- the scene objective
- the buyer question behind it
- what the viewer must understand in a few seconds
- what the scene visually proves
- whether the scene needs text overlay
- whether the scene supports benefit, differentiation, or reassurance

Possible scene types include:
- problem/solution contrast
- product in action
- macro material detail
- gesture or ease-of-use demonstration
- before/after comparison
- size/fit clarification
- product result highlight
- lifestyle use with clear commercial role
- quick proof or reassurance scene

The scene format must serve the commercial message.

## Useful vs decorative scene rule

A scene is useful if it:
- makes the value clearer
- demonstrates how the product works
- proves a claim visually
- reduces a real objection
- helps the buyer imagine real use
- supports preference over alternatives

A scene is weak or decorative if it:
- only adds atmosphere
- delays the product reveal
- repeats information already shown
- depends on vague beauty instead of clarity
- could be removed without reducing sales value

Use `references/useful-vs-decorative-scenes.md`.

## Silent-viewing rule

Assume many viewers will watch without sound.
Therefore:
- the product must be understandable visually
- overlays must be short and readable
- scenes must make the point even without voice-over
- avoid scripts that depend entirely on spoken explanation

## Brief construction sequence

Follow this sequence unless the user explicitly asks for only one subtask.

1. Clarify the product and category
2. Clarify the central promise
3. Identify the main customer problem
4. Rank the top benefits, objections, and differentiators
5. Select 5 to 7 scene messages
6. Assign each scene a role
7. Define what each scene must communicate quickly
8. Choose the best scene type
9. Draft overlays or concise on-screen messages
10. Flag weak or decorative scenes to avoid

## Output style

Use practical, structured outputs with clear headings.

A sensible default output is:
1. Input completeness status
2. Central promise
3. Main customer problem
4. Priority scene messages
5. Scene-by-scene structure
6. What each scene must communicate
7. Suggested overlays
8. Scenes to avoid
9. Final brief or short script ready for production

## Quality checks

Before finalizing, verify:
- the product is understandable very early
- the strongest benefit appears early
- each scene has one dominant job
- the video still works without sound
- differentiation is visible, not just stated
- objections are handled
- the brand supports the sale instead of delaying it
- the user was warned if key strategic inputs were missing

## Mistakes to avoid

- starting with a slow brand intro
- treating the video as a mood piece
- describing features without showing buyer benefit
- repeating the same benefit across several scenes
- relying on voice-over to explain everything
- keeping beautiful but commercially empty scenes
- hiding uncertainty when the brief lacks strategic inputs
