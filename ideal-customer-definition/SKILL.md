# Ideal Customer Definition

Help the user define an actionable ideal customer profile starting from a real product, not from vague audience assumptions.

## Overview

This skill is for product-first customer analysis. Use it to identify the most relevant customer segment for a product, understand why that segment is the best fit, build a persona that is useful for marketing, and turn that persona into practical messaging directions.

This skill is optimized for:
- ecommerce and consumer products
- product positioning and copywriting prep
- persona work that must be directly usable for listings, ads, visuals, landing pages, and messaging

## Core rules

- Start from the real product, its features, use cases, limits, and practical benefits.
- Do not begin with a decorative persona.
- Do not confuse a broad possible audience with the primary ideal customer.
- Translate product features into:
  - concrete benefits
  - customer problems solved
  - customer types most affected
  - likely objections reduced
- Prioritize customer segments before building the final persona.
- Always distinguish:
  - problems
  - frustrations
  - fears
  - desires
  - buying criteria
  - objections
  - action triggers
- Favor useful, actionable analysis over generic marketing language.
- Prefer behavior, context, and buying logic over filler demographics.
- Only include demographic detail when it materially affects the buying situation.
- When information is uncertain, say what is probable versus what is firmly grounded in the product logic.

## Workflow

Follow this sequence unless the user explicitly asks for only one subtask.

1. Clarify the product
2. Identify high-impact features
3. Map each feature to benefit, problem solved, and relevant customer type
4. Rank the most relevant customer segments
5. Select the primary ideal customer
6. Build an actionable persona
7. Derive marketing angles, promises, and messaging directions

If the user already provides a later-stage output, do not restart from scratch. Continue from the highest-quality validated stage already available.

## Stage 1: clarify the product

Collect or infer:
- product name
- main function
- target use context
- important features
- meaningful limits or tradeoffs
- whether the product is premium, budget, specialist, broad-market, or gift-like

If the user provides a product-evaluation file or product notes, treat that as the primary source and extract:
- what the product actually does
- which features matter most to the customer
- which objections the product must overcome
- what differentiates it from weaker alternatives

## Stage 2: identify high-impact features

Do not list every feature mechanically. Focus on features that meaningfully influence:
- interest
- buying confidence
- satisfaction after purchase
- objection handling

For each high-impact feature, map:
- the feature
- the concrete benefit
- the customer problem it helps solve
- the type of person for whom it matters most

Use the feature analysis pattern in `references/analysis-framework.md`.

## Stage 3: rank customer segments

When ranking segments, prioritize by:
- clarity of the problem
- frequency or intensity of the pain
- speed at which the value becomes obvious
- compatibility between product and customer context
- likelihood of a satisfying purchase
- marketing usefulness of the segment

Explicitly separate:
- primary ideal customer
- strong secondary segments
- weaker but still possible buyer groups

Do not say "everyone" or default to an overbroad target.

## Stage 4: build the persona

The final persona must be useful for marketing and sales, not decorative.

Use the structure in `references/persona-structure.md`.

At minimum include:
- profile summary
- life context
- main problems
- daily frustrations
- fears and emotional tensions
- desired outcomes
- buying criteria
- objections
- what this person needs to hear
- action triggers

When useful, indicate:
- what is highly probable
- what is possible but less central
- what is unsupported and should not be overstated

## Stage 5: derive marketing angles

After the persona is solid, derive 3 to 7 marketing angles.

For each angle, include:
- the central idea
- the pain or desire targeted
- the objection reduced
- the message style to use
- the strategic strength of that angle

Then rank the angles and name:
- the best first angle
- the best supporting angle

Use the guidance in `references/marketing-angles.md`.

## Output guidance

Use clear headings and short, practical subsections.

A sensible default progression is:

1. Product analysis
2. Feature-to-benefit mapping
3. Segment ranking
4. Primary ideal customer
5. Persona
6. Marketing angles
7. Recommended next step

When the user asks for only one deliverable, provide just that deliverable, but keep the reasoning product-first and commercially useful.

## Quality checks

Before finalizing, verify:
- the persona comes from the product logic, not stereotypes
- the primary segment is justified against alternatives
- problems, desires, and objections are clearly separated
- the output could directly help with copywriting or positioning
- there is no decorative filler that does not improve decisions

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Building a persona before understanding the product
- Confusing demographics with buying logic
- Targeting too broad an audience
- Mixing problems, desires, and objections together
- Repeating feature lists without translating them into real customer value
- Producing a pretty persona that does not help messaging or conversion

## Example requests

- "Help me define the ideal customer for this product."
- "Rank the best customer segments for my product."
- "Turn this product evaluation into a useful persona."
- "Find the best target audience for this listing."
- "Give me the pains, desires, objections, and marketing angles for this product."
